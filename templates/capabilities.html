// WHY THIS APPROACH?
REASON 01
TRANSFER LEARNING
Training a CNN from scratch on ~500 images would overfit immediately. EfficientNetV2S brings ImageNet-pretrained feature extractors — edges, textures, shapes — that transfer perfectly to aircraft silhouettes, giving a strong starting point with minimal data.
REASON 02
EFFICIENTNETV2S CHOICE
EfficientNetV2S achieves state-of-the-art accuracy-to-parameter ratio. Compared to ResNet50 or VGG16, it is 3–4× more parameter-efficient while matching or exceeding accuracy. Its 384×384 native resolution captures fine structural details like wing sweep and tail configuration — critical for jet classification.
REASON 03
TWO-PHASE FINE-TUNING
Phase 1 trains only the classification head while keeping ImageNet weights frozen — rapidly learning jet-specific patterns without corrupting pretrained features. Phase 2 unfreezes the full network at a low learning rate, allowing subtle domain adaptation. This prevents catastrophic forgetting and leads to better generalisation.

// OBSERVATIONS & ANALYSIS
✔ TRAINING CONVERGENCE
Training accuracy climbs steadily from ~35% at epoch 1 to ~93% by epoch 33, demonstrating the model is effectively learning discriminative features. The two-phase strategy is clearly visible — a smooth rise in Phase 1 (frozen backbone) followed by sharper gains post-epoch 20 as the full network fine-tunes.
⚠ GENERALISATION GAP
A persistent gap between train accuracy (~93%) and validation accuracy (~75%) indicates moderate overfitting. The validation curve plateaus after epoch 20, suggesting the model has saturated on available data. Expanding the dataset per class from ~100 to 300+ images would likely close this gap significantly.
✔ RAFALE — STRONGEST CLASS
The Rafale achieves a perfect 18/18 (100% recall) on the test set with zero false positives. This is likely due to the aircraft's distinctive canard-delta wing configuration being uniquely identifiable compared to the other 4 classes. It was never misclassified in either direction.
✗ SU-57 — WEAKEST CLASS
The Su-57 achieved only 11/20 (55% recall) — the weakest performance. 6 of 20 Su-57 images were incorrectly predicted as F-22, and 2 as Rafale. This is expected: the Su-57 and F-22 share similar twin-tail, large-delta-wing planforms. Visual similarity at certain angles causes the model to confuse the two.
⚠ B-2 CONFUSION WITH F-22
B-2 had 4 misclassifications as F-22 and 1 as Su-57. Given the B-2's radically different flying-wing profile, this may point to dataset issues — web-scraped images with similar background environments or perspective angles creating spurious correlations. Curating higher-quality, diverse-angle B-2 images should help.
◈ LOSS CURVE BEHAVIOUR
Training loss drops sharply from 2.6 → 0.8 within the first 15 epochs, then continues steadily to ~0.46. Validation loss stabilises around 1.0 after epoch 10. The non-converging validation loss (holding at ~1.0 while training loss keeps falling) is the strongest signal of the generalisation gap — a larger dataset or stronger regularisation (L2, dropout) would help.
◈ PHASE 2 FINE-TUNING IMPACT
The dashed Phase 2 boundary at epoch 20 is clearly visible in both charts. Validation accuracy, which had been oscillating around 70–76% in Phase 1, shows improved stability post epoch 20, settling around 74–78%. The spike at epoch 21 (validation drops to ~62%) is a known fine-tuning artefact — the learning rate resets slightly destabilise the network before it recovers. This is expected behaviour and resolves within 2–3 epochs.
